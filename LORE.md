# COREWARD — Story Bible

Canon for everything narrative. Three layers, three delivery channels, one arc:

| Layer | What it explains | How the player gets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmology** — the Lumen Famine | why light is money and worlds are dying | Custodian ruins, glyphs, environment |
| **Humanity** — the drillers before you | who paid for this truth first | wreck logs, salvaged on foot |
| **Ecology** — what lives down there | why the deep is inhabited and hostile | enemies, experienced not read |

The rule for all of it: **the player is never told, only shown, overheard, or dug up.**
No exposition dumps, no quest-giver. Ambiguity is preserved until the last hour.

---

## 1. The cosmology — the Lumen Famine

Light is a substance. It can be drawn, stored, refined, and spent — which is why
the colonies pay in **Lumens (✦)** and why a fuel cell will burn veinlight as
happily as it burns anything else. Ore is valuable because ore carries trace
light; the deeper the seam, the closer to the source, the richer the ✦. The
value curve the player feels in the shop is a diegetic fact.

Roughly twelve centuries ago the stars of this arm began to fail. Not
supernovae — a **drawing down**, slow and quiet, cause unknown and never
explained. An older culture the drillers now call the **Lamplighters** did the
only thing they could think of: they gathered what light remained, broke their
last star — **Aurel** — into fragments, and buried one in the core of every
world that could hold it. A seed bank. Light put away in the dark so that light
could be re-grown when the dark had passed.

Then they built the **Wardens** to guard the vaults, and they died, or left, or
became something else. Nobody knows. Their ruins begin around 600m and are the
only structures down there with right angles.

The worlds grew around what was buried in them. Warmth, weather, oceans,
biology — all of it running on a fragment. **VEIL-3 is not tidally locked.**
That's the survey's lie, or the survey's error. It hangs in permanent amber dusk
because it is a world burning the last of its stored light.

About two centuries ago, **Cindral Extraction** worked out what the fragments
were. Since then, colonies have been founded, drilled, exhausted, and quietly
relocated — the pattern buried under the language of resource depletion. Every
harvested world goes permanently, irreversibly dark.

There were about three hundred seed worlds. **There are three left.**

And because light is the substance of value, the famine is not backdrop — it is
the economy. Everything costs more every year: fuel, heat, passage, plating.
The colonies are poor because the universe is poor. Nobody in this story is
saving up for anything. They are keeping the cold out. **The dying stars and
the poverty are the same event**, and Cindral is the only employer left with
lights on.

## 2. The company

**CINDRAL EXTRACTION** — warm, apologetic, deeply corporate. Cindral does not
lie to you so much as it never quite says the thing. Their recruiting line is
even true: *"Drilling is dangerous. Drilling pays."* They don't need to trick
anyone — in a famine, the dangerous job that pays staffs itself.

Cindral runs the company-town model, and the game's own fees are its ledger:
your pod is leased, your fuel is bought back from the company, the salvage fee
when you die and the tow fee when you run dry are line items in a contract
written to keep a driller almost even, forever. The contract is for ore. Ore is
the cover; ore is a byproduct. What Cindral wants is for someone with no better
option to dig all the way to the bottom, because a survey team down there is a
lawsuit and a driller down there is an accident.

**Dispatch** is the voice on the assay dish: a real person, an operator, chatty
and decent. Their transmissions are the game's emotional barometer — invoicing
and small talk early, hedging in the middle, and by Emberreach they've stopped
reading the script. The last thing Dispatch ever says is not company-approved.

## 3. You

The last contract driller on the Dusklight Rig, flying a leased
**Coreward-class** pod — the cheap deep-hull model, the one they stopped
insuring. You are not a hero, an investigator, or a chosen anything, and you
are not a gold-fever prospector either. **You are needy, not greedy.** The
system is dying, most people are poor, and drilling is the last work that pays.
"One more seam" is not avarice; it is the fuel bill, the repair bill, the lease.

That is the hook the game never lets you off: every truth you uncover, you
uncover because you cannot afford to stop digging. The story indicts the
system, not the driller — but it never pretends the driller's hands are clean
either. Need is how Cindral launders complicity.

## 4. The drillers before you

The wrecks are the human spine of the story. They are seeded by depth, and
**depth is chronology: the deepest wrecks are the oldest.** Recovering them out
of order is the point — the player assembles a conspiracy the way you'd assemble
a shredded document.

Voices already in canon (`config.ts`, `WRECK_LOGS`), to be extended into a real
chronology:

- **K. Vashti** — earliest. Cheerful, doomed, the one who names the pull: *"one
  more seam."* Not gold fever — arithmetic. The refill costs more than the trip
  earned, so the trip gets longer. That arithmetic kills everyone here,
  including, eventually, you.
- **R. Okonkwo** — laughs at a gas pocket. The one who loved it down there.
- **M. Adeyemi** — promised a daughter a piece of the light. The emotional core.
- **The Deep Crew** — six went down on company drills and company debt. The
  debts got paid. Nobody topside believes what paid them.
- **Unsigned** — several. One of them is the driller who worked it out: their
  last three logs are the load-bearing reveal, and they are scattered across
  *different worlds*, so the truth only assembles for a player who travels.

**The hook that turns the screw:** a late wreck carries a log in your own pod's
registry, in your own hand, that you have not written yet.

## 5. The ecology — why anything lives down there

Where light is the scarcest resource in the universe, an ecology will evolve to
eat it. Everything in the deep bands is built around the buried fragment.

- **Veinlight** is not a plant. It is the fragment *leaking* — wild light that
  escaped the vault and grew into something like biology, reaching upward. That
  is why it burns as fuel, why it grows on cave walls in the deep bands, and why
  it thins as you approach Emberreach, where the raw heat kills it.
- **Glimmerflies** eat loose photons. Your headlamp is the brightest meal in the
  stratum.
- **The Long Ones** eat the glimmerflies, and hunt by vibration and heat. They
  move through open passages — including every shaft you have ever cut.
- **Wardens** are not alive. They are Lamplighter machines, twelve centuries into
  a guard duty nobody survived to cancel.
- **The Kindled** are drillers who reached a core and did not come back.

## 6. The fragments

Each world holds one, and each is at a different stage of failure. The
differences are the color script.

| World | Fragment | State | What the world is doing about it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VEIL-3** | The Ember | still burning, still warm | dusk — spending its last reserves |
| **CRYOS-2** | The Glacier Heart | banked, nearly out | froze solid when the light dimmed |
| **MAELIS-6** | The Pearl | encysted | *the ocean is protecting it from you* |
| **(planned)** | — | already taken | a black husk with an intact, empty colony |

**MAELIS-6's twist is the best one available:** a world that layered itself
around its fragment the way a shell layers nacre around a wound. It is not a
hazard. It is a defense, and you are the injury.

**The husk world is the horror beat.** A fourth travel destination that is not a
dig site: a dead planet with a Cindral colony still standing, lights on, nobody
home, and a survey office whose records show the exact same contract you signed.
Reaching it should recontextualize every hour played before it.

## 7. Custodian glyphs — the Nine Stones

Lamplighter ruins carry glyphs: nine carved stones, three to a world, set
into the floors of the halls. **The Lamplighters store precious things
inside worlds** — a star's fragment goes under a mantle, and a message goes
inside the stone that carries its name. Each stone is a door. The vault
beyond it is not somewhere else; it is *in there*, folded into the carving,
larger than the wall that holds it. Dispatch's telemetry blacks out inside,
and her unease is the only narration the vaults ever get.

The vaults are full of **worklight** — the Lamplighters' ambient working
glow, still on. A suit's dry maneuvering jets drink from that air, and
only from that air: light is fuel, money, weaponry, and — in the rooms
they built — lift. Walking a vault to its master stone is what translates
that glyph; the codex is earned line by line, in person. The player learns
to read by going where the words are kept.

At full completion the glyphs resolve into the Lamplighters' final message,
which is not a warning or a curse. It is an **apology**, and a set of
instructions for putting the light back.

The ninth stone names the Kindled: Lamplighters who could not leave the
light they tended, who set down their bodies like tools at the end of a
shift and stayed. The dark, narrow thing inside each figure in the core
chambers is what is left of the body. One of them stands, unlit and inert,
in a vault beneath MAELIS-6 — met before it is ever named.

That message is the gate to the third ending.

## 8. The arc

**Act I — the job (0–2h).** Dig, sell, upgrade. Dispatch is friendly. Assay logs
read like a lonely man's diary. The only strangeness is that the dusk-line is
noted as *creeping*.

**Act II — someone was here (2–6h).** Wrecks. The first Custodian stone: the
deep strata are too regular to be geology. Veinlight is documented as receding
upward year over year. Dispatch begins hedging. You reach the Ember, and it is
beautiful, and something in the chamber knows you.

**Act III — the pattern (6–12h).** The starmap opens. Another world, another
fragment, another set of logs — and the unsigned driller's chronology finally
assembles across two planets. The dish picks up colonies going quiet in
sequence. Wardens appear and they are not hostile *to you specifically*; they
are protecting a vault, which makes you the thief. The husk world confirms it.

**Act IV — the choice (12h+).** Three fragments. Cindral wants all of them.

## 9. Endings

**EXTRACT — the Cindral ending.** Take the last fragment. The colonies get
another century. Three worlds go dark forever. And you — for the first time in
your life — are *out*: debts cleared, lease bought, warm. This ending must be
genuinely tempting, because for a needy driller it is everything the game
taught them to want. As you fly up, the palette drains out of the game — the
color script running backwards — and the final frame is the Dusklight Rig under
a sky with nothing in it.

**RETURN — the Lamplighter ending.** Carry all three fragments back down and put
them where they belong. This costs a needy driller more than it would ever cost
a greedy one: not profit forgone, but the only way out, refused. Your hold is
empty, your tank is empty, your debts are still yours, and you do not come back
up. The last shot is dawn breaking over VEIL-3 for the first time in twelve
centuries: the amber sky the player has stared at for the whole game, finally
turning gold.

**KINDLE — the secret ending.** Requires every glyph, every wreck log, and the
full Ember Forge. Bring the three fragments together in one chamber. They reach
critical mass and reignite — a new star, small and young, in the crust of a
world. This is the ending that ends the *famine*, and therefore the need, and
therefore Cindral. You survive, and you are changed, and the last thing the
game shows you is the Dusklight Rig's contract board, empty, because nobody
has to take that job anymore.

## 10. Delivery channels — the rules

1. **Assay logs** — one per stratum per world, the driller's own diary. Warm,
   personal, unreliable.
2. **Wreck logs** — the human conspiracy, out of order, on foot, on a clock.
3. **Custodian glyphs** — the cosmology, unreadable until earned.
4. **Dispatch transmissions** — the emotional barometer, unskippable, ambient.
5. **The environment** — right angles where there should be none; a light that
   gets *brighter* as you go down.

Never a cutscene. Never a lore NPC. The player should be able to finish the game
having understood almost nothing, and a second player should be able to explain
the entire cosmology from artifacts alone.
