# Polish pass: art direction for the creatures and the Quarters

This adds to ART-DIRECTION.md (above). It covers two things the first pass never reviewed.

## The creatures

These are eight creatures:
- VEIL-3: the Long One, a burrowing serpent; the Geode Mimic, an ore that bites.
- CRYOS-2: Rimewings, Frostbloom.
- MAELIS-6: the Brinewyrm, Pressure Polyps, the Riptide.
- Anywhere: the Kindled, a pursuer made of light.

Each is shown in its own habitat in a creature sandbox. Judge them against `10-creatures.png` and against the house style of the four creatures that were already polished (reference images below):
- **Glow that lands.** A creature's own light spills onto the rock around it, so it is lit by itself in the dark.
- **Readable at play size.** Silhouette, body parts and the dangerous end should read in the gameplay frame, not only in the close-up. A threat must read as a threat.
- **Belongs to its world.** Its colour sits inside its world's hue family (VEIL amber/orange, CRYOS ice-cyan, MAELIS sea-green and nacre), or deliberately against it as an accent.
- **Low-poly, faceted, chunky.** Never realism, never noise.

## The Quarters

The Quarters is the pilot's home on VEIL-3: a side-view hab corridor the suit walks. It runs through the hall first, then two wings bought later, the Gallery (cut stones on plinths) and the Vivarium (glass tanks of creatures remembered as light). Everything is bought from a catalog and appears in a fixed slot:
- rug, arc lamp, potted fern, bunk, galley set, book shelf, radio, star chart;
- four wall palettes: DUSK (stock), SLATEBED, DUST ROSE, VERDANT.

It is the one place in the game with no clock and no danger. It should feel like that. The current room is a flat beige box with a black void for a ceiling, and the player called it basic.

- **A lived-in hab, not a box.** The walls, floor and ceiling should have material and structure: panel seams, ribs, insulated panels, conduit, floor plating, a real ceiling with fixtures, never a black slab. Use value variation across surfaces instead of single flat colours.
- **Light lands, warmly.** The practicals and the arc lamp pool warm light on the wall and floor. Dusk from the portholes spills onto the floor. There are dark corners for contrast, but this is the sanctuary: warm, not grim. Compare the warmth of the lit window in `02-surface.png`.
- **Every bought thing reads as itself and looks cared for.** At play scale and in close-up you can tell:
  - a woven rug (with a pattern);
  - a fern;
  - a bunk with bedding;
  - a kettle and cups;
  - books;
  - a radio, with a glowing dial;
  - a star chart.

  A fully furnished room should look clearly richer than the stock one.
- **Palettes are moods.** Each palette should be a distinct, good-looking room, not just a recolour of one flat surface.
- **The wings are showpieces.** The Gallery lights its stones like museum pieces (pools of light, plinths, glass, placards). The Vivarium's tanks glow with living light that lands on the room.
- **Never trade away:**
  - The suit stays the most legible thing in the frame.
  - The low-poly procedural style stays.
  - Every station and furnishing stays in its slot.
  - The side-view camera stays.
